Four engagements, one method.

Whatever the trigger — an internal transformation, a funding dossier, a tender, or a proof of concept — the same modelling discipline supports the decision.

01

Change management, the LBIP way.

Automation doesn't fail on the technology as often as it fails on the people who have to work differently afterwards. This engagement draws on Airbus's own Lifecycle for Business Improvement Projects (LBIP) — a framework that treats project management, process improvement, and change management as one discipline, not three separate workstreams.

In practice: the as-is diagnosis is built with the people who run the current process, not just about them — so the to-be model reflects real constraints, and the people affected have seen the trade-off before it's presented as a done deal. A model nobody recognises is a model nobody adopts, however correct the numbers.

02

A funding dossier institutions can trust.

Public and institutional funding (France 2030, BPI, regional schemes) increasingly expects a technical case that holds up to scrutiny, not a narrative estimate. This engagement builds the dossier around a modelled trade-off — objectives, as-is, alternatives, quantified to-be — so a reviewer who wasn't in the room for the diagnosis can still be convinced by the case alone.

03

A call-for-tender response that demonstrates.

Where a tender asks for proof of feasibility, a simulated result is evidence a competitor's slide deck isn't. This engagement turns the same modelling discipline used for an internal decision into the tender's supporting case — a demonstrated answer, not an assertion.

04

Feasibility demonstration moving up the TRL scale.

A proof of concept sized to move your project up the Technology Readiness Level scale: from a modelled concept (TRL 2–3) to a demonstration validated in a relevant environment (TRL 4–6). Simulated first, physical where a new process or piece of equipment is involved — so the next investment decision is backed by a demonstration, not a projection.
